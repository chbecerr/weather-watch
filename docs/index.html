    How to read it. The score adds points for cold, heat, wind, rain, snow and thunderstorms, only past a comfort level, so a calm, dry, mild game scores 0. Click a game to see the breakdown. As a rough guide, about 15 points is noticeable (20 mph sustained wind, feels-like 20°F, 1″ of snow), about 40 is severe (30 mph wind, feels-like 5°F, 3″ of snow), and 80 or more is extreme (40 mph wind, feels-like −15°F, 6″ of snow). A ▲ or ▼ shows how much the score has moved since about a day ago as forecasts updated; it appears for moves of 5 points or more, once the site has a few hours of history. To follow a team, open one of its games and tap its star; "Only my teams" then filters the list (saved in this browser only).

    Weeks follow ESPN's schedule weeks, and finished games are dropped. Games with no forecast yet, because they are more than about 7 days out or outside the US, are left out.

    Weather filters show games forecast to have, at some point during the game: wind over 10 mph (sustained), feels-like 32° or colder, feels-like 90° or hotter, 0.05″ or more of rain, 0.1″ or more of snow, thunderstorms, or a 50% or higher chance of precipitation. Pick several to see games matching any of them. The number on each chip is how many of the games you're looking at match it. The chance of precipitation is the Weather Service's, as its highest value during the game; it doesn't change the score.

    Temp is at kickoff; "feels" is the coldest feels-like during the game; wind is the top sustained speed with the top gust beneath it. Forecasts are for the stadium's city and come from the US National Weather Service, so they cover US venues about 7 days ahead and get less certain the further out the game is.
